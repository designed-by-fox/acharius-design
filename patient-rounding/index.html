Modernizing Press Ganey's iRound for hospital rounding

Tackling patient experience through a web-based app for collecting rounding data, deployed in hundreds of hospitals nationwide.

My role
UX Designer at Kin + Carta
Owned the modernization track
Timeline
December 2019 to March 2020
Team
Internal: product director, scrum master, solution architect, UX researchers, product analyst, and me
Client: CIO, director of infrastructure services, client engagement specialist, PM
Platform
Web app used on tablets and desktops

The short version

The challenge

Improve the quality of patient care, leading to higher HCAHPS scores and patient satisfaction, in an app that had never had user research.

What I did

Overhauled the UI and brought it in line with Press Ganey's brand, took part in extensive primary research, and helped sell the client on a direction.

The outcome

A modernized app with a shorter rounding form, patient tabs cut from six to four, and a clear roadmap presented at the final readout.

A north star for rounding

After several iterations with our client, Press Ganey, we landed on a north star for the project:

Through best-in-class user experience, iRound needs to be positioned to enable providers to establish a culture of rounding.

Doing so would increase the flow of high-quality data into the system, which makes iRound more valuable by producing real-time patient insights. From there, our team set two objectives based on our research, project needs, stakeholder values, and timeline:

  1. Encourage use during rounds in a way that enhances human connection, improves patient experiences, and ensures high-quality data.
  2. Help Patient Experience Managers act on real-time data and leading indicators to improve patient experience and health outcomes.

The project began with a scrum master, solution architect, senior product strategist, and me as UX designer. After the first client meetings we identified two tracks: modernizing the existing tool, and exploring new features and experience updates. As the project grew, we added UX research and product.

My role solidified on the modernization track. I worked closely with the UX researchers on interview and usability test questions and heuristics, and with the product strategy and solution development sides to make sure any change was technically feasible.

Research with 34 people, around the holidays

  • 17stakeholder interviews. I conducted 2 and took part in 12.
  • 12user interviews. I took part in 8.
  • 5participants in an on-site contextual inquiry

The research schedule and access were shaped by the time of year, with the winter holidays and New Year's affecting availability, and by how little free time people have in the demanding work culture of healthcare.

Jobs to be done and actor profiles

Because of the scale of use, and how specific each user's tasks were from hospital to hospital, we broke the research into actor profiles with the jobs each one does with patients, other hospital staff, and the iRound app itself. After a few rounds of research and an innovation session with the client, we settled on four.

Key decisions

Decision 1

Design for rounders first

The situation

Four actor profiles, a short timeline, and many competing priorities from stakeholder interviews.

What we chose

The modernization work focused on the Leadership Rounder and Delegated Rounder profiles. Administrators and Data Analysts came second.

Why

Business need, access to interview subjects, timeline constraints, and the most immediate benefit for the fewest technical changes.

Decision 2

Make the rounding form faster to scan

The situation

The rounding form was long and visually hard to parse, which made it harder to round on many patients in a day.

What I chose

Condensed and updated the copy, and made answer options mutually exclusive. Call light use went from "none, 1-3, 3+" to "none, 1-3, 4+".

The result

Buttons could share a uniform size, which sped up visual parsing.

Decision 3

Cut patient tabs from six to four, and rename "Comments"

The situation

Patient information was split across six tabs, and a tab called Comments didn't say whether patients or rounders wrote them.

What I chose

Combined General and Visit Information on one page, restructured the tabs down to four, and renamed Comments to Patient Notes.

The result

It's clear that rounders write these notes, not patients, and there's less clicking between similar tables.

Decision 4

Propose a new pattern for Press Ganey's design system

The situation

On Task Details, the Assigned To row grew very long.

What I chose

Created a new UI pattern to suggest to Press Ganey's design system: multi-select checkmarks with an icon.

The result

Saved significant screen space.

Before and after

To understand every update the design system needed, I did a manual UI audit of iRound and shared the results with external stakeholders. It showed how inconsistent the existing platform was and gave a sense of the effort modernization would take.

Press Ganey had also moved its other products to a unified style guide the year before, and iRound, an acquired product, hadn't caught up. I broke the app down screen by screen to update each interaction and flow.

Research artifacts

We used affinity diagramming to consolidate the stakeholder and user interviews. Each theme played a big role in the experience of using iRound. We prioritized updating the look and feel and the information architecture first. With more time, or more statements of work signed, we could have addressed more of these issues.

Outcome

  • 6 to 4patient information tabs
  • 34research participants, in an app that had never been researched
  • 100sof hospitals using iRound nationwide

Press Ganey didn't end up signing a second statement of work, because COVID intervened. We still left with a clear direction for future feature improvements, which we presented at the final readout.

Reflection

What worked

Reworking the interface to update some flows, the organization of links, and the visual design proved to be the right first step toward modernizing the app.

What was hard

Level-setting with the client on what was possible in a tight timeline was difficult. We had to quickly dissect and prioritize needs, many of them unknown because no user research had been done on the app before. I initially felt lost on where to start, because stakeholder interviews, which came before the user interviews, surfaced so many competing priorities.
